What Paper actually does
Read from their shipping editor bundle (app.paper.design, startRevealAnimation and the agent-highlights HUD layer).
1. It's a choreography with two layers, not one effect. First the piece and up to five of its descendants get outlines in the agent's color, one after another across the first half (750ms). They hold for 200ms, then fade out over 300ms. You see the layout's skeleton before its content.
2. The content "develops" out of the background. The node is visibility:hidden for the first half. A canvas overlay in the color of the nearest filled ancestor, sampled from its fill and not a fixed white, ramps up to opaque over 0 to 750ms. At the halfway mark the node turns visible under the veil, which then thins away top to bottom. It looks like printing or developing a photo, not like a wipe.
3. Nothing moves. No translate, no scale, no blur. Everything lands in its final position, so it reads as calm and "already there."
4. Reads and edits are animated too. A read sweeps a pale highlight upward. An edit or delete does a soft flash over the changed node. The agent's attention is always visible, not only its output.
5. The artboard pulses while the agent works. A 2px stroke breathes from alpha 1 down to .2 on a 3s cycle, plus a "rain" of dots in the artboard's label. It fades in and out over 400ms.
6. It's drawn on a HUD canvas over the DOM, at 2× device pixels and clipped to the nearest clipping ancestor. Re-renders can't interrupt it, and it costs no DOM layers.
Why ours feels flatter
• One beat versus two. Ours is a single wipe. Paper tells a story: "here's the plan," then "here's the thing."
• Our 10px slide moves the layout. On a dense board, things look like they're settling, not appearing.
• The wipe exposes our mask. The hard left-to-right edge reads as a UI transition. Paper's veil is the page's own color, so the content seems to come out of the page.
• The agent is invisible during its write. Paper's outlines carry the agent's color into each element. Ours stays neutral and only the artboard traces yuzu.
